Hello from Silver Browser

A hobby browser engine in Rust. This is about:silver, the browser's own page.

This page is laid out by our own block formatting context — real width/margin resolution and margin collapsing, not a flat vertical list.

Pseudo-elements (M16): this paragraph opens with a drop cap, its first letter floated left and set large by ::first‑letter, and its first line is in capitals by ::first‑line, however wide the window makes it. The cards below are labelled by ::before, from their data‑label attributes, and closed by ::after. Quotation marks come from the quotes property: a quotation, with one inside it, and in French, chosen by :lang(fr), une citation.

Media queries (M20): in a window narrower than 600 pixels the cards below stack in a column, and in one 1000 pixels wide or more the tiles widen. Printed, the page is dark text on white, its cards and tiles bordered rather than filled, and its last paragraphs begin a page of their own.

These cards are flex items (M17): they share a row, grow into the room it leaves, and wrap onto another when the window is too narrow for them, with a gap between them either way.

This card's background and border are painted from real fragment geometry (M6.8) — the box model actually exists now.

This card is 40px tall with overflow: hidden, so this second sentence is clipped away at its padding edge rather than painted over whatever follows. The clip is a rectangle in the display list (M9.3).

This card is 60px tall with overflow: auto, so it scrolls on its own: turn the wheel over it and its content moves inside the clip, with the overlay scrollbar at its right edge showing where it is (M10.5). At the end of its content the wheel goes on to the page, which is scroll chaining.

A second paragraph, to give the card something more to scroll through.

These tiles are grid items (M18): as many columns as the window has room for, by repeat(auto‑fill, minmax(150px, 1fr)), each at least 150px wide and all sharing what is left. A wide tile spans two of them, and dense packing fills the holes the wide ones leave behind.
