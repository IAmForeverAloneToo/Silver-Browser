Page rules

A page is cut where the content no longer fits the space the page area leaves. Lines are never split across pages, so a break falls between two of them. The margins that meet an unforced break are dropped, and the content after it starts at the top of the next page. A printed page has a box, its size on paper, and an area inside its margins where the document is drawn.

A printed page has a box, its size on paper, and an area inside its margins where the document is drawn. Paragraphs flow from one page to the next as they flow down a long screen. Each page shows a slice of the document, and the slices together show all of it. The reference browser prints at three hundred dots to the inch, and so does this engine. A heading that ends a page alone is a poor heading, which is why authors avoid breaks after one. Orphans are the lines a paragraph leaves at the bottom of a page, and widows the lines it carries to the top of the next.

The text on each page is compared with where the reference browser puts it, a run at a time. Fixed boxes are drawn on every page at their place in the page area. When nothing else is possible, the content is broken where the page ends rather than lost. A forced break begins a page, and a break to a side may leave a page blank before it.
